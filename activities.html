LEARN BY DOING

Little learning adventures

Short activities for curious children. Read the steps, make a prediction, observe carefully and talk about what you discover. A grown-up should help with activities involving materials or small objects.

ACTIVITY 1 · WORDS

Become a word detective

Goal: Practise describing words (adjectives).

  1. Choose a familiar object, such as an apple, a cup or a leaf.
  2. Look carefully. What colour is it? What shape is it? How does it feel?
  3. Think of three describing words. For a leaf, you might say “green, thin, smooth.”
  4. Use the words in a sentence: “The smooth green leaf danced in the wind.”

Challenge: Can you describe the same object without saying its name, so someone else can guess it?

ACTIVITY 2 · SCIENCE

Make a colour prediction

You need: washable red and yellow paint, paper and a grown-up's permission.

  1. Before mixing, predict what colour red and yellow might make.
  2. Put a little red and a little yellow paint on the paper or a palette.
  3. Mix them with a brush and observe the result.
  4. Try changing the amount of each colour. Does the shade change?

Think like a scientist: What did you change? What did you notice? Was your prediction correct? Never taste art materials, and clean up with adult help.

ACTIVITY 3 · NATURE

Five-minute nature detective

You need: a window, garden or safe outdoor spot, plus paper and a pencil if available.

  1. Choose one plant or tree to observe from a safe place.
  2. Notice the colours, shapes and textures. Can you spot any insects from a distance?
  3. Draw what you see or write down three observations.
  4. Return another day. What has changed? What stayed the same?

Care for nature: Look without pulling plants apart or disturbing animals. Do not touch unknown insects, mushrooms or plants.

ACTIVITY 4 · CREATIVE THINKING

Invent a helpful creature

  1. Imagine an animal that could help your neighbourhood.
  2. Draw it and give it a name.
  3. Write three facts about it: where it lives, what it eats and how it helps.
  4. Tell a friend or grown-up a short story about your creature.

Extra challenge: Which real animal inspired your invention?
